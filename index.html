About the Project

Green Hydrogen
for Our Community

EcoVolt is a student-led engineering proposal designing a fully integrated green hydrogen production facility — from seawater desalination and electrolysis through to compressed storage, electricity regeneration, and a public fuelling station. Our core mission is to act as a long-duration energy store: capturing surplus renewable electricity as hydrogen and releasing it back to the grid when demand is high.

Core Infrastructure

Explore
the Plant

Our facility integrates four key systems working in sequence to produce, store, and distribute green hydrogen with maximum efficiency and minimal environmental impact.

Core
⚡

PEM Electrolyser

A Proton Exchange Membrane electrolyser splits ultrapure water into hydrogen and oxygen using surplus renewable electricity. High efficiency, fast response to grid signals, compact footprint.

Heat recovery: Waste heat from the PEM stack is captured and used to dry the salt by-product from desalination — turning a process cost into a circular resource.
Upstream
🌊

Water Treatment Plant

We produce our own ultrapure feedwater on-site rather than relying on mains supply. Seawater is drawn in and passed through a two-stage process:

Stage 1 — Desalination: Reverse osmosis removes dissolved salts, producing clean fresh water. The concentrated brine by-product can be dried and sold as industrial salt.

Stage 2 — Deionisation: The desalinated water is further purified by deionisation to remove residual ions, meeting IEC 62282 feedwater specifications for PEM electrolysers.

Any water that doesn't reach the required purity grade can be sold to water companies as clean, pre-treated water — reducing waste to near zero.
Storage
🔒

Hydrogen Storage

Compressed gaseous hydrogen stored in high-pressure vessels on site. Buffer capacity decouples production from demand, enabling hydrogen to be dispatched to either the fuelling station or back to the grid via a fuel cell or turbine as needed.

Offtake
⚡

Power Regeneration & Fuelling

Stored hydrogen serves a dual purpose. Primarily, it can be combusted or fed through a fuel cell to regenerate electricity for the grid during periods of high demand — acting as long-duration storage. Secondarily, a fuelling point provides hydrogen to local fleet operators and fuel cell vehicles.

Oxygen by-product: Oxygen produced during electrolysis is captured (not vented). If certified to medical grade, it can be supplied to local NHS facilities including James Paget University Hospital — creating an additional revenue stream.
How It Works

The Processes

01

Renewable Input

Surplus grid or direct renewable electricity powers the facility, absorbing energy that would otherwise be curtailed.

02

Desalination

Seawater is drawn in and desalinated on-site via reverse osmosis, producing clean feedwater and a salt by-product.

03

Deionisation

Desalinated water is further purified to ultrapure standard before entering the PEM electrolyser stack.

04

Electrolysis

The PEM stack splits H₂O into hydrogen and oxygen gas. Both gases are captured for use — hydrogen for storage, oxygen for potential medical sale.

05

Compression & Storage

Hydrogen is compressed and stored in high-pressure tanks on site, ready for dispatch to grid or fuelling.

06

Power or Fuel

Hydrogen is either converted back to electricity for grid export during peak demand, or dispensed as fuel at our on-site fuelling point.

Regulatory Pathway

Permits &
Approvals

Before construction begins, EcoVolt must secure a series of permits and consents from national and local regulatory bodies. Each approval addresses a different aspect of the plant's operation — from physical construction through to gas safety and water abstraction.

Local Planning Authority

Planning Permission

Approval from Norfolk or Suffolk County Council for the physical construction of the plant, including site access, buildings, and infrastructure.

Environment Agency

Bespoke Environmental Permit

The primary operating licence covering hydrogen production, water treatment processes, and air emissions from the site. This is the most comprehensive permit required.

HSE / Local Authority

Hazardous Substances Consent

Required because the plant will store over 2 tonnes of both hydrogen and oxygen on site. Involves a formal safety consultation with the Health and Safety Executive.

Environment Agency

Water Abstraction Licence

Permission to draw seawater for the on-site desalination unit. Granted by the Environment Agency and subject to environmental impact assessment.

Environment Agency

End-of-Waste Assessment

A formal agreement certifying that the evaporated salt produced during desalination qualifies as a marketable product rather than a controlled waste — enabling commercial sale.

MHRA

Medical Oxygen Authorisation

Licensing from the Medicines and Healthcare products Regulatory Agency to classify and sell oxygen produced during electrolysis as Medical Grade — a prerequisite for NHS supply.

National Grid / DNO

Grid Connection Agreement

A Flexible Connection agreement with the National Grid or local Distribution Network Operator to access curtailed renewable energy and export stored power back to the grid.

HSE

COMAH Notification

Control of Major Accident Hazards — a mandatory safety framework required when storing large volumes of flammable (H₂) and oxidising (O₂) gases on a single site.

Circular Economy

Future
Expansion

EcoVolt is designed to waste nothing. Every by-product and input stream is a potential revenue source — creating a circular economy where the plant's outputs fund its own long-term operation and growth.

By-product Revenue

Industrial Salt Sales

Salt produced during seawater desalination is dried using waste heat recovered from the PEM electrolyser stack — eliminating drying costs. Dry salt is then transported and sold to industry, turning a process by-product into a direct revenue stream.

Waste heat utilised
Healthcare Revenue

Medical-Grade Oxygen

Oxygen produced during electrolysis is captured and, if certified to MHRA medical grade with an oxygen purity analyser on-site, supplied to local NHS facilities. James Paget University Hospital in Gorleston is the primary target customer.

NHS supply pipeline
Water Revenue

Treated Water Sales

Water that has been desalinated but does not meet the full deionisation specification for PEM feedwater can be sold to regional water companies as clean, pre-treated water — rather than being discharged as waste.

Zero liquid waste target
Transport Revenue

Expanded Fuelling Station

As local hydrogen vehicle adoption grows, the fuelling point could be expanded into a full-scale hydrogen refuelling station — comparable to a conventional petrol station — serving passenger vehicles, HGVs, and local fleet operators.

Growth infrastructure
Fleet Contract

First Bus Partnership

First Bus operates local services in the region and is a natural offtake partner for bulk hydrogen supply. A long-term supply agreement would provide revenue certainty and support the decarbonisation of public transport across Norfolk.
